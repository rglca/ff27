# 2026 team fantasy outlooks

## AFC East

### Buffalo Bills

Buffalo remains one of the best PPR environments because Josh Allen supplies rushing and passing value while James Cook has a strong path to volume, but the receiver room is now more crowded around DJ Moore, Khalil Shakir, and Keon Coleman than it was when Allen concentrated targets in one or two players. Watch whether Cook keeps the goal-line work and whether Moore becomes the clear first read; that determines whether the team supports multiple weekly starters or just a high-end RB plus volatile WRs. The early waiver names are Ray Davis and Ty Johnson if Cook misses time, while a later-season target-share winner among Shakir/Coleman/Moore could become more valuable than his draft slot. Shakir and Coleman carrying current question marks make the lower-ADP depth worth monitoring, but do not overreact to one preseason absence. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Allen/Cook and the current receiver competition.

### Miami Dolphins

Miami has a very fantasy-friendly run-game shape when De'Von Achane is healthy, especially in PPR where his receiving role can compensate for a lighter rushing workload, but the current quarterback and receiver picture is much less bankable than the recent Miami versions. The big watch item is whether Malik Willis can sustain enough pass volume and whether Jaylen Wright or Ollie Gordon earns meaningful complementary work; otherwise Achane may be carrying the entire fantasy case. Wright is the early waiver priority if Achane is unavailable, and Jalen Tolbert, Caleb Douglas, or Malik Washington are the later names to watch if one earns a stable route share. The risk is a low-volume, spread-out passing offense that hides a useful secondary receiver until the usage becomes obvious. [PFN's Aug. 29 chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) shows Achane as the clear backfield centerpiece but an unsettled quarterback/receiver room.

### New England Patriots

New England is more attractive in PPR if Drake Maye's development produces a high-volume, mobile passing game, with A.J. Brown offering a strong target anchor and the Rhamondre Stevenson/TreVeyon Henderson backfield potentially giving the offense both early-down and receiving value. The central watch is Henderson's ankle and whether he earns enough passing-down work to matter alongside Stevenson; if that split is real, both backs become more fragile weekly plays than their individual talent suggests. Henderson is the obvious early waiver or contingency stash if his draft cost stays depressed, while Romeo Doubs and the next wideout to win the third role are later-season adds. The team has upside, but the quarterback is still young and Henderson's health creates a lower-ADP backfield option that can be easy to miss. [PFN's current depth chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Maye, Stevenson, Henderson, and Brown together.

### New York Jets

The Jets look like a middling PPR environment: Garrett Wilson can command targets, but Geno Smith's ceiling and a Breece Hall-led backfield with Hall currently questionable make the weekly floor less stable than the player names suggest. Watch whether Hall returns to full work, whether Braelon Allen gets passing-down snaps, and whether the offense can generate enough red-zone possessions to support more than Wilson. Allen is the clearest early waiver add if Hall misses time, while Adonai Mitchell and the secondary receivers are later names if the route distribution changes. Hall's recurring availability questions are exactly what keep Allen's ADP low; draft Hall for the upside but keep the handcuff and injury news close. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) has Hall questionable, Allen behind him, and Wilson as the established receiver.

## AFC North

### Baltimore Ravens

Baltimore remains one of the strongest fantasy systems because Lamar Jackson's rushing gives the quarterback an elite PPR floor and Derrick Henry converts the team's physical identity into touchdown and yardage equity, but the passing pie can be narrow and volatile behind Zay Flowers. Watch Flowers' current undisclosed issue, whether Rashod Bateman keeps a full-time route role, and how much Mark Andrews is used near the goal line; a healthy Flowers-Andrews pairing can support multiple starters, while an injury opens a much clearer target path. Justice Hill and Rasheen Ali are the early contingency backs, and whichever secondary receiver or tight end gains snaps is the later waiver target. The main risk is Henry's age and the possibility that concentrated touchdowns mask thin week-to-week receiving volume. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Flowers as questionable behind a Lamar/Henry core.

### Cincinnati Bengals

Cincinnati is one of the cleanest PPR systems when Joe Burrow is healthy: Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins can both win targets, and Chase Brown brings useful receiving potential from the backfield. The watch item is Chase's knee designation and whether the team keeps enough tempo and pass volume to support a third fantasy contributor beyond the two stars. Samaje Perine and Tahj Brooks are the early waiver backs if Brown is unavailable, while Andrei Iosivas or Colbie Young become immediate later adds if Chase or Higgins misses time. Chase's current question mark is the kind of news that can create a temporary buying window, but the risk is paying full price before the practice reports settle. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) has Burrow/Brown and lists Chase questionable.

### Cleveland Browns

Cleveland is a lower-confidence PPR environment because the quarterback competition around Deshaun Watson, Shedeur Sanders, and Dillon Gabriel makes pass volume and efficiency difficult to project, even though Quinshon Judkins and Harold Fannin Jr. offer interesting workload paths. Watch who actually starts, whether Judkins owns the backfield or shares with Dylan Sampson, and whether Jerry Jeudy can become the clear first read instead of competing with the rookie wideouts. Sampson is the early waiver back, while KC Concepcion, Denzel Boston, and Fannin are the later names to monitor for route growth. The risk is an offense that creates only one reliable fantasy starter; Judkins' value is also partly concealed by the unclear quarterback situation rather than a clean projection. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) shows the unsettled quarterback room and the Judkins/Fannin core.

### Pittsburgh Steelers

Pittsburgh has a possible PPR rebound if Aaron Rodgers can keep the offense on schedule and feed DK Metcalf and Michael Pittman, but the current quarterback and receiver health tags make this more of a situation to monitor than a system to trust blindly. Jaylen Warren and Rico Dowdle could split meaningful work, which helps the weekly floor only if the Steelers create enough plays and red-zone chances. Kaleb Johnson is the early waiver stash if the backfield changes, while Roman Wilson or Germie Bernard are later adds if either questionable veteran receiver misses time. Metcalf and Pittman are the lower-ADP players whose upside is hidden by availability uncertainty; the risk is a low-efficiency offense where target volume does not become PPR points. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Rodgers, Warren, Dowdle, and both receiver question marks.

## AFC South

### Houston Texans

Houston is a good but concentrated PPR environment: C.J. Stroud and Nico Collins form the stable passing spine, while David Montgomery and Woody Marks create a backfield with touchdown opportunity but uncertain reception distribution. Watch whether Montgomery owns short-yardage work, whether Marks earns third downs, and which receiver emerges behind Collins; the second pass-catcher is likely to be a better waiver target than a preseason-ranked name. Woody Marks is the early add if Montgomery misses time, and a healthy Tank Dell-type return or whichever young receiver earns full routes is the later swing. The risk is an offense that looks efficient but funnels fantasy value into Collins and the RB split, leaving the rest matchup dependent. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) shows Montgomery/Marks and Collins at the top of the current skill-position groups.

### Indianapolis Colts

Indianapolis has a friendly PPR shape if Daniel Jones is competent because Jonathan Taylor can dominate touches while Josh Downs, Keenan Allen, Alec Pierce, and Tyler Warren give the offense several ways to distribute volume. The watch is health: Pierce, Downs, and Warren are all carrying current designations, so the final route and red-zone hierarchy may look very different by Week 1. DJ Giddens is the early waiver priority behind Taylor, while Ashton Dulin, Nick Westbrook-Ikhine, or Mo Alie-Cox become later adds if the injured pass-catchers miss time. Taylor is the dependable anchor, but the receiver values are risky until the quarterback and the question marks resolve. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Taylor and a crowded, injury-flagged passing group.

### Jacksonville Jaguars

Jacksonville could be a useful PPR offense if Trevor Lawrence sustains a higher-tempo passing game, with Brian Thomas Jr., Parker Washington, and Jakobi Meyers offering target volume while Bhayshul Tuten and Chris Rodriguez compete for backfield touches. Watch whether Tuten becomes the true lead back, whether Rodriguez owns goal-line work, and how quickly Thomas returns to full contact after his shoulder issue. Rodriguez is the early waiver pivot if the backfield splits or Tuten misses time, while Parker Washington and the next full-time receiver are later adds. Thomas' health and the unsettled RB hierarchy are the main risks, but they also conceal the value of the lower-ADP backups. [PFN's current depth-chart hub](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) is the best place to monitor the Tuten/Rodriguez and Thomas situations.

### Tennessee Titans

Tennessee is a lower-end PPR environment until Cam Ward proves the offense can create consistent volume, and Tony Pollard/Tyjae Spears may both need receiving work to overcome a possible touchdown shortage. Carnell Tate is the player to watch for a larger target share, but the current chart suggests a young passing group without an established fantasy floor. Spears is the early waiver add if Pollard's workload or health slips, while Nicholas Singleton and the next receiver to earn snaps are later stashes. The risk is drafting a name from an offense that cannot sustain enough drives; Pollard's workload could still be useful, but Ward's development is the swing factor. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Ward, Pollard, Spears, and Tate in the current starting picture.

## AFC West

### Denver Broncos

Denver has a promising PPR blend if Bo Nix continues to use his legs and the offense supports multiple pass-catchers, with Courtland Sutton, Jaylen Waddle, and Evan Engram giving it a strong top-end target mix. The risk is the J.K. Dobbins/RJ Harvey split and the possibility that Waddle or the tertiary receivers reduce each other's weekly volume; watch routes, red-zone snaps, and whether Harvey takes the passing-down role. Harvey is the early waiver player if Dobbins' workload or health wobbles, while Troy Franklin, Marvin Mims, and Jaleel McLaughlin are later adds if their roles expand. Dobbins' health volatility is the main reason Harvey can be more valuable than his draft rank suggests. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Nix, Dobbins/Harvey, Sutton, Waddle, and Engram.

### Kansas City Chiefs

Kansas City is still structurally fantasy-friendly because Patrick Mahomes can create volume and Rashee Rice has a path to high-value targets, but both Mahomes and Kenneth Walker are currently questionable and the backfield behind Walker is thin. Watch whether Walker participates fully, whether the Chiefs' receiver rotation produces a dependable second option, and whether the tight end room recaptures its old red-zone concentration. Brashard Smith and Jaydn Ott are the early waiver backs, while the first young receiver or tight end to earn a full-time route rate is the later add. Walker's health is particularly important because the current depth chart offers less obvious replacement production than his ADP implies. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Mahomes and Walker as questionable and Rice as the lead receiver.

### Las Vegas Raiders

Las Vegas has a fragile PPR outlook: Kirk Cousins can support a useful target earner, but Ashton Jeanty's ankle issue and a thin backfield make the offense highly sensitive to early-season health and role news. Watch whether Jeanty returns to full work, whether Tre Tucker or Jalen Nailor wins the highest-value routes, and whether the quarterback creates enough scoring chances for the offense to support more than one weekly starter. Mike Washington Jr. and Dylan Laube are the early contingency names if Jeanty misses time, while the receiver who gains red-zone work is the later waiver target. Jeanty's current question mark hides the value of his low-ADP replacements, but the overall risk is that Vegas lacks enough volume to make the handcuff more than a short-term play. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) shows Jeanty questionable behind Cousins.

### Los Angeles Chargers

The Chargers remain a reasonably strong PPR system because Justin Herbert should sustain passing volume and Ladd McConkey has a clean target path, while Omarion Hampton projects as the lead back in an offense that can score. Watch whether Hampton receives goal-line and receiving work, how much Quentin Johnston actually plays, and whether Keaton Mitchell's current question mark changes the backfield rotation. Kimani Vidal and Jaret Patterson are the early waiver backs to know, while the third receiver or tight end who earns a full-time role is the later add. The main risk is that the Chargers spread short-area work across too many players, but Herbert gives the top options a stable weekly floor. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Herbert, Hampton, McConkey, and Mitchell's status.

## NFC East

### Dallas Cowboys

Dallas is a highly attractive PPR offense if Dak Prescott stays healthy because CeeDee Lamb and George Pickens can both command volume and Javonte Williams gives the backfield a usable touch projection. The watch is whether Pickens' presence pulls Lamb down from elite target concentration and whether the Cowboys use a second back or Jaydon Blue in passing situations. Blue is the early waiver back, while Ryan Flournoy, KaVontae Turpin, and Jake Ferguson are later adds if either starting receiver or the tight end misses time. Pickens' arrival creates a real weekly ceiling but also introduces target-competition risk; Dallas' lower-ADP backups are worth tracking because the offense should generate fantasy-relevant opportunities. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Prescott, Williams, Lamb, and Pickens together.

### New York Giants

The Giants have a higher-upside PPR profile than the team total may suggest if rookie Jaxson Dart can feed Malik Nabers, but the backfield is crowded around Cam Skattebo, Najee Harris, Devin Singletary, and a questionable Tyrone Tracy Jr. Watch Nabers' knee status, Dart's willingness to throw downfield, and whether Skattebo or Tracy becomes the clear receiving back. Singletary and Tracy are early waiver names depending on the Week 1 rotation, while Darius Slayton or the next wideout to win snaps is the later add. Nabers and Tracy's health uncertainty conceals the handcuff value, but the quarterback floor is the reason to keep expectations measured. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) shows Dart, Skattebo, Tracy, and Nabers' current question mark.

### Philadelphia Eagles

Philadelphia is one of the strongest PPR systems because Jalen Hurts' rushing and red-zone role raise the team ceiling, Saquon Barkley can dominate touches, and DeVonta Smith has a reliable target path even when the offense is balanced. The watch is whether the Eagles keep a true second receiver on the field and how much Tank Bigsby or Will Shipley matters behind Barkley; a Barkley injury would create an immediate high-value waiver situation. Bigsby and Shipley are the early contingency names, while Dontayvion Wicks or Makai Lemon are later adds if their route shares jump. The risk is that rushing touchdowns and target distribution make the offense better for its stars than its depth, but the system is still highly amenable to fantasy. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Hurts, Barkley, Smith, Wicks, and Lemon.

### Washington Commanders

Washington has a useful PPR shape with Jayden Daniels supplying rushing upside and Terry McLaurin, Deebo Samuel, Rachaad White, and Jacory Croskey-Merritt offering multiple volume paths, though the current RB room is already carrying several question marks. Watch whether Croskey-Merritt or White controls early downs, whether Daniels keeps enough designed runs, and whether Samuel is used as a true receiver or gadget piece. Kaytron Allen and Jeremy McNichols are the early waiver contingency backs, while the third receiver to earn consistent routes is the later add. White and Croskey-Merritt are lower-ADP players whose value is obscured by the current injury tags; the risk is role fragmentation around Daniels rather than a lack of fantasy-friendly talent. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Daniels and the question-marked backfield.

## NFC North

### Chicago Bears

Chicago is a rising PPR environment if Caleb Williams takes a step, with D'Andre Swift, Rome Odunze, Luther Burden III, and Colston Loveland giving the offense several high-volume paths, though the current chart already flags Burden and Kyle Monangai. Watch whether Swift keeps passing-down work, whether Burden is full-time or eased in, and whether Loveland and Kmet split enough routes to cap both tight ends. Roschon Johnson or Monangai is the early waiver back if the rotation changes, while Kalif Raymond or the next receiver behind Odunze/Burden is a later add. The risk is young-quarterback variance and a crowded target tree; Burden's health is especially important because it can push a lower-ADP secondary receiver into immediate PPR relevance. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Williams, Swift, Odunze, Burden, and Loveland.

### Detroit Lions

Detroit is one of the best fantasy systems in the league: Jahmyr Gibbs has elite rushing and receiving upside, Amon-Ra St. Brown owns a stable target role, and Jared Goff supports efficient scoring, but the return of Isiah Pacheco and Sam LaPorta's current hip issue could alter the red-zone and short-area distribution. Watch Pacheco's actual role, LaPorta's practice participation, and whether Jameson Williams keeps enough volume to justify his explosive weekly ceiling. The early waiver names are Pacheco's replacement and the backup tight end if LaPorta misses time, while Kalif Raymond-type depth becomes a later add only if the wideout rotation changes. Detroit's risk is more about touchdown and target competition than offensive quality. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Gibbs, Pacheco as questionable, and LaPorta's current status is worth following.

### Green Bay Packers

Green Bay should remain a good but spread-out PPR offense with Jordan Love, Josh Jacobs, Jayden Reed, Christian Watson, and rookie Matthew Golden all carrying plausible weekly roles. The watch is Jacobs' current question mark, Watson's availability, and whether Golden or another young receiver becomes Love's preferred downfield option rather than a rotational player. MarShawn Lloyd and Chris Brooks are the early waiver backs if Jacobs misses time, while Golden or Savion Williams are later adds if the route tree consolidates. The main risk is too many capable pass-catchers and a backfield split; Jacobs and Watson's health volatility is what keeps lower-ADP replacements interesting. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Jacobs questionable with Watson/Reed/Golden in the receiver mix.

### Minnesota Vikings

Minnesota has a strong top-end PPR setup with Justin Jefferson, Jordan Addison, and an offense now led by Kyler Murray, but the RB room around Aaron Jones and Jordan Mason is more likely to be a committee than a clean bell cow. Watch Murray's rushing usage, whether Addison remains a full-time player, and whether Mason gets goal-line or short-yardage work as the season develops. Mason is an early waiver or handcuff priority if Jones misses time, while the later add is whichever third receiver or tight end claims the open volume. The risk is age and workload management in the backfield, but Jefferson gives the team a much safer passing-game floor than most committee offenses. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Murray, Jones, Mason, Jefferson, and Addison.

## NFC South

### Atlanta Falcons

Atlanta is an attractive PPR system if Tua Tagovailoa or Michael Penix Jr. settles the quarterback situation, because Bijan Robinson can score in every phase and Drake London has a clear target ceiling while Kyle Pitts remains a useful middle-of-field option. Watch which quarterback starts, whether Brian Robinson Jr. takes enough red-zone work to damage Bijan's touchdown ceiling, and whether Jahan Dotson becomes a real third option. Tyler Goodson and the next receiver to win consistent routes are the early waiver names; Zachariah Branch is a later upside stash if his role grows. The quarterback competition and Penix's current designation create more risk than the skill-position talent alone suggests. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists the QB competition and the Bijan/London/Pitts core.

### Carolina Panthers

Carolina is a middling PPR environment with a clear target leader in Tetairoa McMillan but an unsettled backfield around Chuba Hubbard and Jonathon Brooks, plus Bryce Young still needing to prove he can support multiple fantasy starters. Watch Hubbard's hamstring, whether Brooks is eased into a committee, and whether the pass volume reaches the 2025 level implied by McMillan's current market. The early waiver names are the healthy Panthers back behind Hubbard/Brooks and Jalen Coker if he becomes a full-time receiver, while Trevor Etienne or the second tight end is a later add if the staff changes the rotation. Hubbard's status conceals Brooks' contingent value, but the main risk is committee usage in an offense that may not create enough touchdowns. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Hubbard questionable with Brooks behind him.

### New Orleans Saints

New Orleans has a better PPR path than its team reputation if Tyler Shough can keep the offense functional and Chris Olave remains healthy, but the running back room is volatile around Travis Etienne, a questionable Alvin Kamara, Kendre Miller, and questionable Devin Neal. Watch whether Etienne gets receiving work, whether Kamara's availability changes the split, and whether Olave's high target share carries over with the new quarterback. Kendre Miller and Devin Neal are the early waiver names if Kamara or Etienne misses time, while the secondary receiver who wins routes is the later add. The risk is low efficiency and too many backfield mouths, but Kamara's availability is exactly what can hide a potentially useful lower-ADP replacement. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) shows Shough, Etienne, Kamara's question mark, and Olave.

### Tampa Bay Buccaneers

Tampa Bay offers a usable PPR system if Baker Mayfield continues to push tempo and Mike Evans, Chris Godwin, Bucky Irving, and Emeka Egbuka all keep meaningful roles, though the team has enough receiver and backfield overlap to create weekly volatility. Watch Evans' groin, Egbuka's health, and whether Irving is the clear receiving back or shares more work than expected. The early waiver priority is the next back behind Irving if his workload changes, while Jalen McMillan or the receiver who wins the third route package is a later add. Evans and Godwin are the veteran values whose availability can reshape the entire target tree; the risk is that injuries and rotation keep every secondary player matchup dependent. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) and [RotoWire's current news feed](https://www.rotowire.com/football/news.php) are the two places to monitor the receiver health picture.

## NFC West

### Arizona Cardinals

Arizona has a concentrated PPR appeal around Trey McBride and Marvin Harrison Jr., while rookie Jeremiyah Love and James Conner offer upside if either becomes the clear lead back, but the quarterback room around Jacoby Brissett and a questionable Carson Beck lowers the offense's ceiling. Watch Love and Conner's availability, whether Michael Wilson stays ahead of the secondary receivers, and whether Arizona creates enough pass attempts to support a third option. Tyler Allgeier and Bam Knight are the early waiver backs if the injury flags turn into missed time, while Wilson or Kendrick Bourne are later adds if the target hierarchy changes. Conner and Love's health uncertainty is why the lower-ADP backfield options deserve attention; McBride is the safest weekly anchor. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists the Brissett/Beck situation and the Love/Conner question marks.

### Los Angeles Rams

The Rams are one of the most fantasy-friendly environments when Matthew Stafford is healthy because Puka Nacua and Davante Adams can absorb heavy target volume and Kyren Williams has a strong role in a concentrated offense. The watch is Nacua's groin/psoas issue, Adams' workload, and whether the young pass-catchers behind them earn enough routes to matter; current chart depth at tight end also carries question marks. Blake Corum is the early waiver or handcuff priority if Kyren misses time, while Terrance Ferguson and the receiver who steps up behind Nacua/Adams are later adds. Nacua's health is the biggest draft risk, but it also creates a clear discount and a possible immediate opportunity for the Rams' lower-ADP depth. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Nacua questionable with Williams/Corum behind him, and [RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/football/player/puka-nacua-16790) has the current player-level context.

### San Francisco 49ers

San Francisco remains a high-quality PPR system when Christian McCaffrey is available because the offense creates efficient RB touches and concentrated targets, but McCaffrey, Mike Evans, and the broader receiver room are all carrying enough health uncertainty to make the depth chart essential. Watch McCaffrey's return to team drills, whether Evans' groin issue clears, and whether Christian Kirk or De'Zhaun Stribling earns the third receiving role. Jordan James and Kaelon Black are the early waiver backs, while Stribling, Demarcus Robinson, or the next receiver to inherit snaps are later adds. The key risk is paying for peak 49ers efficiency while assuming the stars will stay healthy; the current lower-ADP replacements are more valuable than the board may show. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists McCaffrey/Evans as questionable and shows the current contingency players.

### Seattle Seahawks

Seattle has a strong top-end PPR setup with Sam Darnold feeding Jaxon Smith-Njigba, but the backfield is already in flux with Zach Charbonnet listed out and Jadarian Price positioned to gain work. Watch whether Price takes passing downs, whether Cooper Kupp and Rashid Shaheed form a stable second/third receiver rotation, and whether the tight end group supplies usable red-zone volume. Price is the obvious early waiver add while Charbonnet is out, while Kupp's health and whichever secondary receiver earns full routes are later adds. Charbonnet's status hides Price's contingent value, and the risk is that Seattle's target tree remains too concentrated in JSN for the rest of the room to have a stable PPR floor. [PFN's current chart](https://www.profootballnetwork.com/nfl-hq/depth-charts) lists Charbonnet out with Price behind him and JSN atop the receiver room.
